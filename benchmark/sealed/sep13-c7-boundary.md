The pre-cutoff operator definition reported in `cases/EXTRA-LEARNABLE-GAIN.md` source `codex:01a03d28-4c3b-70a0-94f0-d4396cde19b6:000244` is, row by row,

For finite `S = Σ_j |â_j|`, the actual row mass is `m = ρmax S/(1+S) < ρmax`. Setting `ρmax=1.00` **still guarantees `m<1`**. Thus C7 P1–P4 and P6 can test whether the *0.95 safety margin* helps versus a looser subunit constraint; they cannot test whether a strict subunit constraint itself is necessary. Checking that rows exceed 0.95 under the 1.00 arm confirms that the margin changed, not that the operator became noncontractive. Literal equality to either cap is impossible at finite `S`.

Even a positive result for 0.95 with such crossings would compare a tighter cap with a permissive operator; it would not by itself isolate a singular threshold effect from changed amplitude or optimization. A tie/loss with verified crossings would show that strict subunit mass was unnecessary for the observed task performance in that recipe.
